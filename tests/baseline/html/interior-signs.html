Planning Interior Signs for an NYC Office

Planning indoor business signs for an NYC office starts with the building, then the layout, then the look.

Building Management Approval

Ask management first. Interior signs usually depend on approval from building management or the landlord, and lit signs need a licensed electrician. We prepare drawings and plan the install around your building's rules.

ADA Requirements

Know which signs need tactile text. Under the ADA Standards, signs for permanent rooms and spaces need raised characters and Braille, mounted 48 to 60 inches above the floor beside the latch side of the door. Directional signs follow visual rules. See our ADA signs page.

Keeping Each Sign in One Design

One style across each floor. Matching fonts, colors, materials, and mounting heights make a building read as one place, even when you order signs in stages.

Dimensional Letters or a Panel System

Letters for statement walls, panels for signs that change. Choose dimensional letters for a logo or mission wall that stays put, and a panel system for directories and room signs that update often.

What Clients Say

Common Questions

Should every room use the same sign?+

Use one design style, and vary the sign type by purpose. Matching fonts, colors, materials, and mounting heights keep a building consistent, while room signs, directories, and wall graphics each follow their own rules. Permanent room signs need tactile text and Braille, and directional signs do not.

Can existing signs be kept and worked into the new design?+

Often yes. Send us photos of your existing signs and our team will assess their condition and fit before we advise. If a sign stays, we match new signs to its finish and style, and we plan any removal and wall patching in your quote.

What material is best for a lobby sign?+

Acrylic and brushed metal are two popular choices. Acrylic offers glossy color and supports backlighting, while brushed metal provides a refined look that holds up well in busy areas. The best choice depends on your wall, your lighting, and your brand. See our lobby sign cost guide for price differences.

Do interior signs need building management approval in NYC?+

Usually yes. Building management or the landlord typically approves tenant signs before work starts, and many buildings set rules for materials, mounting, and work hours. Lit signs need a licensed electrician. We prepare the drawings management asks for and plan the install around your building's rules.

Which interior signs need Braille and tactile characters?+

Signs that identify permanent rooms and spaces, such as room numbers and restrooms, need raised characters and Braille under the ADA Standards. Directional and informational signs need legible visual characters but not tactile text. Tactile signs mount 48 to 60 inches above the floor, beside the latch side of the door.

Can you install after hours?+

Yes. We schedule installs around your building's rules, including evenings, weekends, and freight elevator windows. After-hours work can add labor charges, and we confirm them in your quote. Licensed installation partners handle the install.

Do you work in all five boroughs?+

Yes. We serve all five boroughs, and each building has its own access rules and approvals. Tell us your address when you request a quote, and we will plan the schedule, permits, and crew for your site.
