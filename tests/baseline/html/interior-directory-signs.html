Types of Directory Signs

A building directory sign lists where tenants and departments are. The right type depends on how often names change and how many people cross your lobby each day.

TypeBest useHow tenant names get changedTypical materials
Lobby directoryThe main entrance of an office or multi-tenant buildingReplace the inserts, or the whole panel, through our teamAluminum frame, acrylic face, painted finish
Floor directoryThe elevator landing on each floorSwap the inserts for that floor's tenantsAluminum frame, acrylic or painted panel
Tenant panels with changeable insertsBuildings with frequent tenant turnoverSlide out one strip and slide in a new oneAluminum frame with engraved or printed inserts in acrylic, metal or vinyl
Digital directoryLarger lobbies that want live updatesUpdated from a content system. Ask our team.A display in a housing. Ask our team.

Most commercial building directory systems pair a lobby directory with a building floor directory on each landing, so visitors can find a tenant at the entrance and confirm the stop when the elevator opens. Plan any office building directory with turnover in mind.

Built for Tenant Turnover

Tenants move in and out, so a directory should change without replacing the sign. Tenant directory signs read best when the whole system is planned for change. See signs for offices and properties.

Changeable Inserts

Swap one strip and leave the frame in place. Tenant names sit on inserts that slide out of the frame, so move-outs are a quick change.

Replacement Inserts

Order the next strip when a tenant changes. Send us the new name and suite, and our team prepares a matching insert.

Matching the Building

New inserts match the frame and the lobby. We keep the font, color, and finish consistent, so the directory still looks like part of the building.

List Order

List the building name first, then each tenant with its suite or floor, in one consistent order. Alphabetical is easiest to scan.

Materials and Mounting

Aluminum frames, acrylic faces, and painted finishes cover most lobbies. Wall-mounted directories suit an entry wall or corridor, and freestanding directories suit an open lobby.

Frames and Faces

Aluminum frames withstand daily handling, and acrylic faces take color cleanly. Painted finishes let the directory match your lobby.

Mounting

Wall-mounted units save floor space, and freestanding units work where no wall is available. We confirm the mounting method against your wall and your building's rules.

What Changes the Price

Listing count, frame size, material, mounting method, and inserts all affect the price. A building directory board with many listings needs a larger frame. Standard small directories start in the low hundreds of dollars online, and custom lobby directories cost more.

ADA and Accessibility

Directories are informational signs. ADA rules require directional and informational signs to have legible visual characters, a non-glare finish, and clear contrast; they are not required to be tactile.

Room ID signs for permanent spaces follow tactile and Braille rules. See our ADA signs page, and ask our team about the requirements for your building. Tell us the finish and contrast your building needs, and our team confirms the layout before production.

Directories as Part of a Full Lobby Package

A directory works best as the first step in a larger wayfinding system. Plan directory signs for the lobby and each floor together, so the whole building shares one design.

A full package can include:

Common Questions

How do we update tenant names?+

Slide out the old insert and slide in a new one. Changeable directories hold each tenant name on its own strip, so a move-out is a quick change and the frame stays in place. Send us the new name and suite, and our team prepares a matching insert.

How long does a directory take to make?+

Most directories take two to three weeks from approved drawing to install, plus shipping time from our Salt Lake City production facility and any building approval. Large lobby directories and digital options can take longer. Tell us your target date, and we will confirm the schedule in your quote.

Can a new directory match our existing lobby sign?+

Yes. Send photos of your lobby sign, and our team matches the font, color, and finish so the directory looks like part of the same design. If the original sign is hard to reference, we work from your photos and brand files.

Do you offer digital directories?+

Our team can advise on digital directories. Vision Graphics provides digital signage consulting, installation, and content creation, so we can talk through whether a digital directory suits your building. Send us your lobby layout and tenant count, and we'll review the options.

Can you install after hours?+

Yes. We schedule installs around your building's rules, including evenings, weekends, and freight elevator windows. After-hours work can add labor charges, and we confirm them in your quote. Licensed installation partners handle the install.

What should a building directory list?+

Start with the building name, then list each tenant or department with its suite number or floor, in one consistent order. Alphabetical order is easiest to scan. Floor directories repeat only that floor's listings, so visitors can confirm their stop when the elevator opens.

What affects the cost of a directory sign?+

Listing count, frame size, material, mounting method, and the number of inserts all move the price. Small standard directories start in the low hundreds of dollars online, and custom lobby directories cost more. We itemize each quote, so you can see what drives the price.
