Storefront Sign Types

Exterior business signs work well as a set: a main sign above the door, an eye-level sign, and window details. Our storefront signs cover seven common options, and we can combine them in one design.

Channel Letter Signs

Individual letters mounted on the facade, lit or unlit. They read from across the street and carry your name after dark. Learn more about channel letter signs in NYC.

Dimensional Letter Signs

Raised letters or a raised logo on the sign band or wall. They give a clean, architectural look in daylight. Learn more about dimensional letter signs.

Cabinet (Lightbox) Signs

A lit box with a printed face, mounted on the facade. You can replace the face when your artwork changes, and landmark districts may limit signs lit from inside. Learn more about cabinet signs.

Blade Signs

Double-sided signs that project from the facade over the sidewalk. People walking the block see them before they see a flat sign. Learn more about blade signs.

Building Signs

Larger signs for upper walls and taller facades. They suit buildings with several tenants and signs that must read from farther away. Learn more about building signs.

Window Graphics

Vinyl lettering and graphics on the glass for hours, services, and door details. They support the main sign at eye level. Learn more about window graphics.

Panel and Metal Signs

Flat aluminum or metal panels carrying the name and logo, for wide sign bands and plain facades. They give a solid, simple look. Learn more about metal signs.

Some storefronts also carry awning signs. If your building has an awning, our team can plan the new sign around it.

NYC Permits, Landlords and Landmark Districts

Your sign may need approvals before it goes up. The rules below are the city's, and they may change, so check them early in your plan.

DOB Sign Permit

A sign permit is generally required for a sign display or installation. A non-lit sign of about six square feet or less, or a painted sign, may need none. Zoning rules still apply.

Electrical and Illuminated Sign Permits

A lit sign needs a separate electrical permit filed by a licensed electrician. A lit sign that extends beyond the building line may also need an annual illuminated sign permit.

Zoning Size Limits

Your zoning district sets limits on sign area and lighting. In C1 and C2 districts, lit signs may total up to three times your street frontage in feet, with a 50-square-foot cap. Non-lit signs follow the same three-times rule, with a 150-square-foot cap.

Licensed Sign Hangers

A licensed sign hanger performs or supervises most sign installations and removals. Exemptions cover signs up to 75 square feet and 25 pounds, and a registered general contractor may install non-lit business signs with limited text. A licensed sign hanger must have a place of business in NYC.

Landlord Approval

Your lease may set sign rules, and your landlord or building management may need to approve the design before work starts. Send any lease sign criteria early, and we will prepare the drawings they ask for. We can provide a certificate of insurance upon request, and our team confirms your building's requirements.

Landmark Districts and LPC Review

In a landmark or historic district, LPC reviews exterior signs. Staff cannot approve exterior signs lit from inside, which includes front-lit channel letters. Halo-lit solid letters with a concealed light source may qualify if they meet the pin-mounted limits.

Local Law 15 of 2026

DOB will not issue work-without-a-permit violations for certain accessory signs, including business storefront signs, that existed on or before February 9, 2025. That runs through February 8, 2028, with temporary assistance through August 7, 2028. It does not make a noncompliant sign lawful and does not cover a new replacement sign.

Permit paperwork is part of the plan our project lead manages. Ask our team who files for your sign and which approvals apply at your address.

Common Questions

How much does a storefront sign cost in NYC?+

Most storefront signs cost roughly $1,500 to $12,000 or more for the sign itself, depending on type, size, and lighting. A typical NYC channel letter set runs about $4,000 to $12,000. Installation is priced separately. Treat these as planning ranges that your quote replaces.

Do I need a permit for a storefront sign in NYC, and do you handle it?+

Often yes. DOB generally requires a sign permit, and a non-lit sign of about six square feet or less, or a painted sign, may need none. Lit signs also need an electrical permit. Our project lead manages the paperwork, and our team confirms who files for your address.

How long does it take from approval to installation?+

Most signs take 1 to 5 weeks to make and ship after you approve the drawing, plus time for city review. Simple graphics take 1 to 2 weeks, dimensional or metal signs 2 to 4, and lit signs 3 to 5. A full channel letter project runs about six to ten weeks.

Can you remove my old sign as part of the job?+

Yes. Removal is part of the install plan. Wall patching and paint touch-up are included in your quote, and our team will confirm the details. Send a photo of your old sign and the surrounding facade, and we'll plan the removal with the new sign.

Do you install early morning or after hours?+

Yes. We schedule installs around your building's rules, including early mornings, evenings, and weekends. After-hours work can add labor charges, and we confirm them in your quote. Licensed installation partners handle the installation, and our project lead manages the schedule.

Which boroughs do you cover?+

We serve all five boroughs: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island. We make signs in Salt Lake City and install them through licensed partners, with one Vision Graphics project lead as your contact. How big can a storefront sign be in NYC?+

Your zoning district sets the limit. In C1 and C2 districts, lit signs may total up to three times your street frontage in feet, capped at 50 square feet. Non-lit signs follow the same rule, with a 150-square-foot cap. Your lease and any landmark rules can set lower limits, and our team checks your district.

Can I light my sign in a landmark district?+

Possibly, with limits. LPC staff cannot approve exterior signs lit from inside, which includes front-lit channel letters and cabinet signs. Halo-lit solid letters with a concealed light source may qualify if they meet the pin-mounted limits of 18 inches tall and 2 inches deep. Other proposals may go to a public hearing.

Does Local Law 15 cover my existing sign?+

It may. Local Law 15 of 2026 pauses work-without-a-permit violations for certain accessory signs, including business storefront signs, that existed on or before February 9, 2025, through February 8, 2028. It does not make a noncompliant sign lawful and does not cover a new replacement sign. Ask our team to review yours.
