Types of Office Signs

Office signage and suite signage fall into six groups. Pick the signs you need, or plan them together so fonts, finishes, and mounting heights match. A complete office signage plan keeps each floor easy to read.

Door and Nameplate Signs

Names and titles at the entrance to each office. Staff names change often, so these are built to be updated. Learn more about door signs.

Room ID Signs

Room numbers and names for conference rooms and shared spaces. Conference room signs and room ID signs keep a floor easy to read.

Wall and Logo Signs

Brand walls and suite signs that carry your logo. These office signs for wall displays work at reception, suite entrances, and shared areas, and business wall signs can match across floors. Learn more about lobby signs.

Directory Signs

Tenant and floor lists with changeable inserts. Directories keep multi-suite floors easy to navigate. Learn more about directory signs.

Directional Signs

Arrows and floor markers that point to rooms and exits. They guide visitors without a stop at the front desk. Learn more about directional signs.

ADA and Restroom Signs

Tactile room labels and restroom signs. Permanent rooms need raised characters and Braille where such signs are provided. Learn more about ADA signs | Learn more about restroom signs

Planning Signs for a New Office or Move

Count the rooms before you count the signs. A short list at the start keeps the whole system consistent.

  • Sign count: each room, entrance, and corridor that needs a sign
  • Naming: final room names, room numbers, and staff names
  • ADA rooms: which signs identify permanent rooms and need tactile text
  • Brand style: fonts, colors, finish, and logo files
  • Building rules: certificate of insurance, approval, work hours, and freight elevator

Your building and fit-out team sets code-required signs, such as exit, stair, evacuation, and restroom signs. Share that list early so the office signs match.

Common Questions

What signs does an office need?+

Most offices need four groups: a brand sign at reception, room ID signs, wayfinding signs and directories, and the safety signs your building requires. Plan them as one system so fonts, finishes, and mounting heights match. Your building and fit-out team sets exit, stair, and evacuation signs.

Can name signs be changed when staff moves?+

Yes, when the sign has a replaceable name insert. Occupant names are exempt from the ADA sign rules, so you can change an insert while the room number stays in place. Send us the new name and our team will prepare a matching insert.

Do room signs need to be ADA compliant?+

Where room signs are provided, the ADA Standards require raised characters and Grade 2 Braille on signs that identify permanent rooms, such as room numbers, conference rooms, and restrooms, mounted 48 to 60 inches high on the latch side. Directional signs need visual rules only, and occupant names are exempt. See our ADA signs page.

How long does it take?+

Most office signs take two to three weeks from approved drawing to installation, plus shipping time from our Salt Lake City production facility and any building approval. Large multi-floor packages can take longer. Tell us your move-in date, and we will confirm the schedule in your quote.

Can you work after hours?+

Yes. We schedule installs around your building's rules, including evenings, weekends, and freight elevator windows. After-hours work can add labor charges, and we confirm them in your quote. Licensed installation partners handle the install.

Can you plan signs for several floors or suites?+

Yes. We plan the whole set together, quote it by floor, and phase installs with your building so each floor is finished before the next begins. Send us your floor plans and room list, and our team will prepare one drawing set for each floor.
