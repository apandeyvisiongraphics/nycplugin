### 5. H2: Room ID and ADA Signs → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Room signs need clear names and a plan for the applicable standards. The ADA Standards set the rules, and your design professional confirms what applies.
- `h3` <strong>Permanent Room Signs</strong>
- `p` Permanent room signs need raised characters and Braille, where provided, under the ADA Standards. Classroom door signage with a room number and a name is common. See <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/door-signs/">door signs in NYC</a></strong>.
- `h3` <strong>Directional and Informational Signs</strong>
- `p` Directional and informational signs need only visual criteria: contrast, a non-glare finish, and legible characters. Placement still affects how well people find their way.
- `h3` <strong>Updating Names</strong>
- `p` Room names change with programs and staff. Ask <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">our team</a></strong> about updateable options.
- `h3` <strong>Standards Review</strong>
- `p` Your school's design professional confirms the standards for each sign. We state the rules and do not certify compliance. See <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/ada-signs/">ADA signs in NYC</a></strong>.
