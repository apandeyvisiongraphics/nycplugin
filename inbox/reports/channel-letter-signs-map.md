### 3. H2: How Channel Letters Are Built → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Each letter is its own shallow metal can with a face, a return, LEDs, and a back. Outdoor sign letters built this way are made for night visibility.
- `h3` <strong>Faces</strong>
- `p` The face is the lit or solid front of the letter. Front-lit letters use translucent acrylic, and halo-lit letters use a solid face.
- `h3` <strong>Returns</strong>
- `p` The returns are the sides, usually aluminum. They add depth and side color to each letter.
- `h3` <strong>LEDs</strong>
- `p` LED modules sit inside each letter. LED channel letters run on a power supply, and even module spacing keeps the face evenly lit.
- `h3` <strong>Backs</strong>
- `p` The back closes the letter and carries the mounting. Front-lit letters have an opaque back, and halo-lit letters have a clear back that lets the light wash the wall. Trimless channel letters leave off the trim cap for a cleaner edge.

Halo-lit letters are also called reverse channel letters, and halo-lit signage pairs a solid face with a glow behind it. Backlit or illuminated channel letters come in all four styles.

### 5. H2: Mounting Options → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` How the letters attach to your facade changes the look, wiring, and permit drawings.
- `h3` <strong>Raceway</strong>
- `p` A metal box behind the letters that houses the wiring and power supply. Raceway signage simplifies the electrical hookup, and channel letters on raceway mounts suit uneven walls or buildings with limited drilling.
- `h3` <strong>Flush (Direct) Mount</strong>
- `p` Letters attach directly to the wall, with wiring routed behind. Flush mounting gives the cleanest look on solid masonry, and it needs precise wall penetrations.
- `h3` <strong>Backer Panel</strong>
- `p` Letters mount to a flat panel that mounts to the wall. A backer panel evens out a rough surface and can make removal easier at the end of a lease.

### 8. H2: NYC Permits and Approvals → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Lit channel letters are regulated in New York City. Your sign may need several approvals, and the list depends on its size, lighting, mounting, and location.
- `h3` <strong>Sign Permit</strong>
- `p` A lit sign may require a DOB sign permit. Lit channel letters are illuminated signs, so plan for one.
- `h3` <strong>Electrical and Illuminated Sign Permits</strong>
- `p` A lit sign may require a separate electrical permit filed by a licensed electrician. A lit sign that extends beyond the building line may also require an annual illuminated sign permit.
- `h3` <strong>Zoning</strong>
- `p` Your zoning district sets how large a sign can be and how it may be lit. We check the rules for your address before the design is final.
- `h3` <strong>Landmarks and Historic Districts</strong>
- `p` LPC staff cannot approve internally illuminated exterior signs, including front-lit letters. They may approve halo-lit letters with solid faces and a concealed light source, pin-mounted up to 18 inches tall and projecting no more than two inches.
- `p` Standard channel letter depths may exceed that limit, and other designs may need a public hearing, so ask our team before you design.
- `h3` <strong>Landlord and Building Approval</strong>
- `p` Your landlord may require approval of the drawings, and some leases set their own sign rules. Send your lease's sign guidelines early.
- `h3` <strong>Licensed Installation</strong>
- `p` A licensed sign hanger may be required to install or supervise the work, with exemptions for small signs. Our licensed installation partners handle the filing and installation, and our team prepares the drawings.
- `h3` <strong>Existing Signs and Local Law 15 of 2026</strong>
- `p` NYC has temporary protections through February 8, 2028 for certain accessory signs that were in place by February 9, 2025. They do not make a noncompliant sign lawful, and they do not cover a new replacement sign. Ask our team about your letters.

### 13. H2: Common Questions → `faq` (existing markup/CSS)

- `p` <strong>How much do channel letter signs cost?</strong>
- `p` Most channel letter signs for an NYC storefront cost roughly $4,000 to $12,000, and small sets start near $3,000. Halo-lit letters typically cost about 15% to 30% more than front-lit, and combination letters about 25% to 40% more. Letter height drives the price the most, and we itemize each quote.
- `p` <strong>Do I need a permit?</strong>
- `p` Usually yes. A lit sign may require a DOB sign permit and a separate electrical permit, and a lit sign that extends beyond the building line may also need an annual illuminated sign permit. Zoning applies, and landmark districts add review. We prepare the drawings and coordinate the licensed filer.
- `p` <strong>Front-lit or halo-lit?</strong>
- `p` Front-lit letters give the highest night visibility and are simpler to service. Halo-lit letters give a refined glow on the wall behind them, and they may be easier to approve on a landmarked building. Combination letters give both. Send a photo of your facade, and we will recommend one.
- `p` <strong>How long do the LEDs last?</strong>
- `p` Quality LED modules are rated around 50,000 hours to 70% of their original brightness, which is about 12 years at 12 hours a day. The power supply can wear out sooner, so periodic checks keep the sign lit evenly. <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">Ask our team</a></strong> about maintenance and coverage for your letters.
- `p` <strong>How long does it take?</strong>
- `p` Most projects run about six to ten weeks from approved drawing to installation, including permits. Fabrication and shipping take about three to five weeks, and permit and landmark review vary by sign and address. Tell us your target date, and we will confirm the schedule in your quote.
- `p` <strong>Can you repair or replace existing letters?</strong>
- `p` Yes, our team can assess them and advise if a repair or a replacement makes sense. A replacement sign is new work that may need its own permit, and Local Law 15 of 2026 protections do not cover a new sign. <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">Send us photos of your letters</a></strong> to start.
- `p` <strong>Can channel letters go on a landmarked building?</strong>
- `p` Sometimes. LPC staff cannot approve internally illuminated exterior signs, which includes front-lit letters. They may approve halo-lit letters with solid faces and a concealed light source, pin-mounted up to 18 inches tall and projecting no more than two inches. Other designs may need a public hearing, so ask our team first.
