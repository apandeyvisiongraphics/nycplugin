### 5. H2: NYC Permits and Building Approval → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` New York City regulates exterior signs. Your sign may need several approvals, and the list depends on its size, lighting, mounting, and location.
- `h3` <strong>Sign Permit</strong>
- `p` Most exterior signs may require a DOB sign permit. A non-illuminated sign of six square feet or less generally does not, but zoning still applies.
- `h3` <strong>Electrical and Illuminated Sign Permits</strong>
- `p` A lit sign may require a separate electrical permit filed by a licensed electrician. A lit sign that extends beyond the building line may also require an annual illuminated sign permit.
- `h3` <strong>Zoning</strong>
- `p` Your zoning district sets how large a sign can be and how it may be lit. We check the rules for your address before the design is final.
- `h3` <strong>Landmarks and Historic Districts</strong>
- `p` Signs on landmarked buildings or in historic districts may require written approval from the Landmarks Preservation Commission. Those rules often favor non-lit or halo-lit lettering.
- `h3` <strong>Landlord and Building Approval</strong>
- `p` Your landlord or building management may require approval of the sign drawings, and some leases set their own sign rules. Send your lease's sign guidelines early.
- `h3` <strong>Licensed Installation</strong>
- `p` A licensed sign hanger may be required to install or supervise the work, with exemptions for small signs. Our licensed installation partners handle the filing and installation, and our team prepares the drawings. See how <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/sign-installation/">sign installation in NYC</a></strong> works.
- `h3` <strong>Existing Signs and Local Law 15 of 2026</strong>
- `p` NYC has temporary protections through February 8, 2028, for certain accessory signs in place by February 9, 2025, and DOB encourages owners to bring existing signs into compliance before then.
- `p` The protections do not make a noncompliant sign lawful, and they do not cover a new replacement sign. Ask our team about your sign.

### 6. H2: Built for New York Weather → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` New York weather is hard on exterior signs: sun, freeze-thaw cycles, wind-driven rain, and road salt.
- `h3` <strong>Materials</strong>
- `p` Powder-coated aluminum holds up well outdoors. UV-stable faces resist fading, and stainless hardware resists rust.
- `h3` <strong>Lighting</strong>
- `p` LED modules in outdoor business signs should be rated for outdoor use. Sealed enclosures keep moisture out of lit signs, and a licensed electrician makes the final connection.
- `h3` <strong>Maintenance</strong>
- `p` Plan a periodic check. Clean the faces, check the mounting hardware, and replace failed lighting before it becomes a safety issue.
- `p` A common rule of thumb for size is about one inch of letter height per 10 feet of viewing distance. We confirm the size on a drawing of your facade.

### 11. H2: Common Questions → `faq` (existing markup/CSS)

- `p` <strong>Do I need a permit for an exterior sign in NYC?</strong>
- `p` Usually yes. Most exterior signs may require a DOB sign permit, and a lit sign may also need an electrical permit and, if it extends beyond the building line, an annual illuminated sign permit. A non-illuminated sign of six square feet or less generally does not, but zoning still applies. We prepare the drawings and coordinate the licensed filer.
- `p` <strong>Lit or non-lit?</strong>
- `p` Lit signs read after dark and suit storefronts open in the evening, but they may need electrical and illuminated sign permits. Non-lit signs involve fewer permits and less upkeep, and landmark districts often favor non-lit or halo-lit lettering. Tell us your address and hours, and we will recommend one.
- `p` <strong>How long does it take?</strong>
- `p` Most exterior signs take three to five weeks to fabricate and ship, plus DOB permit review and, where it applies, landmark review. Permits often set the schedule, so we start them early. Tell us your target date, and we confirm the timeline in your quote.
- `p` <strong>Do you install?</strong>
- `p` Yes. Licensed installation partners install your sign, with one Vision Graphics project lead managing the process. They handle the filing and installation, and our team prepares the drawings and coordinates the schedule, including after-hours work. We tell you who will be on site before installation day.
- `p` <strong>Can you replace an existing sign?</strong>
- `p` Yes. Removal is part of the install plan. A replacement is new work and may need its own permit, and the city's temporary protections for existing signs under Local Law 15 of 2026 do not cover a new sign. <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">Send us a photo of your sign</a></strong>, and we will plan both steps.
- `p` <strong>What type of exterior sign is best for an NYC storefront?</strong>
- `p` Match the sign to who sees it. Channel letters suit storefronts open after dark, a blade sign catches pedestrians on busy blocks, and a fascia sign suits a clean facade. In landmark districts, non-lit or halo-lit lettering is often easier to approve.
