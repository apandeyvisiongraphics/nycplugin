### 5. H2: Working in Manhattan Buildings → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Many Manhattan sign delays come from building paperwork and access. These nine points cover what to settle before production.
- `h3` <strong>Building Management Approval</strong>
- `p` Property teams often review a sign drawing before work starts. Send us the building's sign standards early, and we'll prepare drawings in the required format.
- `p` Many tower management offices keep a standing process for contractor paperwork, so ask for it at the start.
- `h3` <strong>Certificate of Insurance</strong>
- `p` Managers often ask for a certificate of insurance before a contractor enters the building. We can send one on request, and our team confirms the wording your building needs.
- `h3` <strong>Freight Elevators and Access</strong>
- `p` Many Manhattan buildings book freight elevators or loading docks in set windows. Tell us your building's booking steps, and we plan the schedule around them.
- `h3` <strong>After-Hours Installs</strong>
- `p` Tower rules often push installs to evenings or weekends. Work outside business hours can add labor charges, and your quote lists them. Read more about <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/sign-installation/">sign installation in NYC</a></strong>.
- `h3` <strong>Landmark Districts and LPC Review</strong>
- `p` Parts of Manhattan sit in landmark districts, where LPC reviews exterior signs. Staff cannot approve exterior signs lit from inside, so front-lit letters and lightboxes may need a hearing or a different design.
- `p` Pin-mounted halo-lit letters with a concealed light source may qualify under LPC's staff criteria.
- `h3` <strong>Street-Level Shops in Mixed-Use Buildings</strong>
- `p` Many Manhattan storefronts sit under offices or apartments so that a sign may touch the facade, the shared entrance, and the landlord's rules at once. Ask who controls the facade before design starts.
- `h3` <strong>Narrow Blocks and Deliveries</strong>
- `p` Manhattan blocks pack in narrow sidewalks, scaffolding, and delivery zones, so an install needs a plan for where the crate goes and when. Tell us about sidewalk sheds or street rules at the address.
- `h3` <strong>What Affects a Manhattan Quote</strong>
- `p` Size, materials, mounting height, wall or facade condition, and old-sign removal all move the price. Restricted work hours and building access add to the scope.
- `h3` <strong>What to Send for Your Building</strong>
- `p` Send the address, photos of the wall or facade, rough sizes, and any building sign standard. Add the property manager's contact if you have it. See the <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/resources/project-brief/">sign quote checklist for NYC</a></strong> for the full list.

- `p` Yes, on request. Property managers often ask for a certificate before a contractor enters, and each building writes its own requirements. Send those early, and <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">our team</a></strong> confirms what we can provide for your property.
- `p` <strong>Do you install after hours in occupied buildings?</strong>
- `p` Yes. We plan installs around the building's rules, which often mean evenings or weekends in occupied towers. Work outside business hours can add labor charges, and your quote lists them. Licensed installation partners install the signs, and your project lead coordinates access.
- `p` <strong>What is your typical lead time?</strong>
- `p` Lead time depends on the product. Simple graphics take about 1 to 2 weeks from approved drawings; lobby and office signs take 2 to 3 weeks; and lit exterior signs take 3 to 5 weeks to make and ship. Building approvals, city review, and shipping from Salt Lake City add time.
- `p` <strong>Do you handle sign permits in Manhattan?</strong>
- `p` Exterior signs may need a DOB sign permit, and landmark districts add LPC review. Small non-lit signs of about six square feet or less may need no DOB permit. Paperwork runs through your Vision Graphics project lead, and <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">our team</a></strong> confirms the filing party for your address.
- `p` <strong>Do I need landmark approval?</strong>
- `p` Possibly, for exterior signs in a landmark district. LPC staff cannot approve exterior signs lit from inside, and vinyl on storefront glass is capped at 20 percent of the glazed area. <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/contact/">Our team</a></strong> checks if your address sits in a district.
- `p` <strong>How do freight elevators and building access work?</strong>
- `p` Many Manhattan buildings book freight elevators or loading docks in set windows. Tell us the building's booking steps and any delivery rules, and we plan delivery and installation around them. Your project lead coordinates access with the property team.
- `p` <strong>Where are your signs made, and who installs them in Manhattan?</strong>
- `p` We produce signs at our Salt Lake City facility, and we ship the finished signs to your building. Licensed installation partners install them, and one Vision Graphics project lead is your contact from drawing to installation. Shipping time from Salt Lake City is part of your quote.
- `p` <strong>How do I start a Manhattan sign project?</strong>
- `p` Send the building address, photos of the space, and any building sign standards through the quote page. Add the property manager's contact if you have it. Our team replies with questions and next steps, and a drawing follows once the details are clear.
- `p` <strong>Which sign companies handle office lobby signs in Midtown Manhattan?</strong>

- `p` Licensed installation partners handle installation, with one Vision Graphics project lead overseeing the job. Share a photo of the lobby wall, and our team will send a drawing and a quote.
