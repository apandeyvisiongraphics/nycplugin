### 3. H2: Wayfinding vs Directional Signs → `rich-text` (new section template)

- `p` A directional sign points the way at a single decision point, such as a corridor junction. Wayfinding is the whole system: destination names, numbering, sign types, and the route between them.
- `p` Directional signs are one part of it, so plan the system first and then place each sign where a visitor must choose.
- `p` Wayfinding design starts with the route. Wayfinding systems keep names, arrows, and numbering consistent from the entrance to the door.

### 5. H2: Directional Sign Formats → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Indoor directional signs in NYC come in several formats, and each suits a different sightline. Custom directional signs can match your building's finishes, so match the format to the corridor, the ceiling, and the pace of foot traffic.
- `h3` <strong>Wall-Mounted Directional Signs</strong>
- `p` Directional signs with arrows sit at corridor junctions and lobby decision points. In NYC buildings, directional wall signs work at a height people can read while walking.
- `h3` <strong>Projecting and Flag Signs</strong>
- `p` A flag sign stands out from the wall so people see it down a corridor. Under the ADA Standards, wall-mounted objects between 27 and 80 inches may project only 4 inches.
- `h3` <strong>Overhead Signs</strong>
- `p` Overhead directional signage helps in open lobbies and wide corridors where wall signs are hidden. The ADA Standards call for at least 80 inches of clearance below them.
- `h3` <strong>Floor Graphics</strong>
- `p` Floor graphics guide people through open areas and queues. See <strong><a href="https://www.visiondoesit.com/nyc/floor-graphics/">floor graphics in NYC</a></strong> for options.
- `h3` <strong>Temporary Route Signs</strong>
- `p` Temporary route signs direct events, moves, and short-term changes. Plan the material and the removal around the display dates.

### 6. H2: Planning a Wayfinding System for an NYC Building → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` A wayfinding signage plan starts with the route and ends with a sign schedule. These five steps keep the plan consistent from the entrance to the door.
- `h3` <strong>Walk the Route as a First-Time Visitor</strong>
- `p` Trace the journey from each entrance to the main destinations and note each unclear turn. Ask someone who does not know the building to try it too.
- `h3` <strong>Name Each Destination Once</strong>
- `p` Use one name for each room, department, and tenant on all signs. Two labels for one place send visitors the wrong way.
- `h3` <strong>Plan Floor and Suite Numbering</strong>
- `p` Floor and suite numbers need a logical order that signs and directories repeat. Share your numbering plan early so the sign schedule matches it.
- `h3` <strong>Plan for Tenant Changes</strong>
- `p` List what changes often, such as tenant names and meeting room names. Updateable components may suit those, and fixed panels suit stable destinations.
- `h3` <strong>Review Accessibility Early</strong>
- `p` Ask your design professional which ADA Standards apply, then share the specification with us. Directional signs need visual criteria, and permanent room signs need raised characters and Braille.
- 🖼 **Image** (3:2)

### 7. H2: Exit and Safety Signs in NYC → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Visitor wayfinding and life-safety signs do different jobs. NYC rules cover exit and egress markings, and they may change.
- `h3` <strong>Exit Path Markings</strong>
- `p` High-rise office buildings may need photoluminescent exit path markings in approved materials under Local Law 26 of 2004. These markings help people find the way out when lighting fails.
- `h3` <strong>Approved Materials</strong>
- `p` NYC lists approved products, and shipments need manufacturer certification. The building owner handles the filings.
- `h3` <strong>What We Supply</strong>
- `p` Our team confirms which safety signs we supply for your building. Send your building's exit sign requirements early so we can plan visitor wayfinding and safety signs together.

### 8. H2: Materials and Updateable Systems → `features-grid` (new section template)

- `p` Choose materials by what changes. Permanent destinations suit fixed panels, and changing names suit updateable components.
- `h3` <strong>Fixed Panels</strong>
- `p` Acrylic, aluminum, and photopolymer panels suit stable destinations such as stairs and main corridors. They keep a consistent look across a building.
- `h3` <strong>Updateable Components</strong>
- `p` Updateable components let you change a name without replacing a whole sign. Our team confirms the options and how replacements are ordered.
- `h3` <strong>Finish and Brand Match</strong>
- `p` Finish and color should match your building's standards across all signs. Send brand guidelines or photos of existing signs so new signs sit beside them.
